Linamar (LNR.TO) — The Clear Winner

The Canadian government explicitly named Linamar as a preferred JV partner. Already operating in China, 34,000 employees globally, C$10B in annual revenue, ~8.5x earnings. At this valuation, it's not expensive despite the run. As BYD and Chery finalize assembly plans — Minister Joly met both in January — Linamar is first in line for order flow acceleration. Accumulate on pullbacks to C$85–C$90.

Magna International (MGA) — Scale + Dividend

Magna is Tier 1 at scale — any Canadian EV production flows through its supply chain. Down from $69 highs, the entry here is better than January. Pays a modest ~3% dividend while you wait for JV news to crystallize. P/E of 19.8 reflects its size; the risk is legacy Detroit exposure as Chinese imports take share. Accumulate at current levels; add on weakness to $55.

BlackBerry / QNX (BB) — Mispriced Optionality

Security concerns about Chinese software? QNX is the answer. A Canadian OS running in 255 million vehicles — including Chinese brands Geely, Dongfeng, and GAC — it gives Ottawa political cover while giving Chinese engineers a platform they already know. BB is down 28% over the past year; the market treats it as a legacy tech story. Recent wins include the Haleytek partnership for Volvo's EX60 EV SUV. If Canada formally designates QNX as the "security buffer" for Chinese JVs, the stock re-rates instantly. Speculative allocation: 5–10%. Add on weakness to $3.00.
